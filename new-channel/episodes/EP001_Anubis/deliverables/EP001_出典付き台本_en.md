## 00:01 N01 The god beside the scale

区分：myth_variant

A heart is placed on a scale. On the other side sits a symbol of what is right and true. Standing beside the scale is a god with the head of a canine: Anubis. He is famous as the fearsome judge of the dead. But look closely at the ancient sources, and you will find that Anubis does not judge alone.

区分：historical_fact

## 01:27 N05 Canines and cemeteries

区分：scholarly_interpretation

The museum's description draws attention to the link between canines and cemeteries. Animals that roamed burial grounds could disturb the dead. Yet a watchful canine could also be imagined as a power that guarded them. This is an interpretation of the image's background, not proof of a single origin for Anubis.

## 02:41 N08 Preparing for the journey

区分：myth_variant

One of Anubis's most important roles was his connection to mummification. In ancient belief, preparing the body of the deceased and performing rituals helped sustain their existence after death. Preserving the body was not only about appearance. It was tied to the hope of living again.

- [S03 Nedjemankh's Gilded Coffin Exhibition Gallery](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2018/nedjemankh-gilded-coffin/exhibition-gallery) — Nedjemankh Becomes a Mummy; burial rites and embalming scene

## 03:01 N09 A god protecting the body

区分：myth_variant

A gilded coffin made for a priest named Nedjemankh was once displayed at the Met. The museum's description points to a scene of a mummy on a bier, with Anubis performing rites beside it. Isis and Nephthys also appear as protectors of the mummy. The coffin was later found to have been looted, and in 2019 it was returned to Egypt.

区分：historical_fact

The idea of protection appears beyond statues of the god, too. The ritual object called an imiut combines a staff, a jar, and a form wrapped in an animal skin. According to the museum, objects like this and guardian figures were connected to sacred spaces and funerary rites. Protecting the dead took many forms.

## 04:09 N12 Opening the Book of the Dead

区分：historical_fact

Now we move from preparing the body to the story of the journey after death. Here we meet the Book of the Dead. The museum notes that its ancient name was the Book of Going Forth by Day. It is a collection of spells and images on papyrus, meant to help the deceased on their way.

- [S04 Book of the Dead for the Chantress of Amun, Nauny](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/548344) — Description; Spell 125 scene; date; museum audio transcript

## 04:34 N13 A papyrus for one person

区分：historical_fact

On screen now is a papyrus that accompanied the burial of a woman named Nauny. The museum dates it to around 1050 BCE. The scroll holds images and texts for several spells, and among them is the scene of the weighing of the heart. It was made not for an abstract dead person, but for one particular individual.

- [S04 Book of the Dead for the Chantress of Amun, Nauny](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/548344) — Description; Spell 125 scene; date; museum audio transcript

## 04:58 N14 What the heart carried

区分：myth_variant

Why the heart? Writing about another funerary papyrus, the museum explains that the heart was considered the center of intellect and emotion. In the weighing scene, how a person had lived is what is being tested. The heart was shown not as a mere organ, but as a record of a life.

Let's look again at Nauny's papyrus. Anubis is reading the scale. But he is not alone in the scene. There is a god who records the result, and a god who receives the dead. Once we separate these roles, the idea of Anubis as a judge who decides everything on his own turns out to be too simple.

区分：myth_variant

On this fragment of cartonnage, Thoth, with the head of an ibis, holds writing equipment. The museum describes the scene as Thoth introducing the deceased to Osiris. The clue to identifying a god is not just the shape of the head. What the god holds, and what the god is doing, matter just as much.

## 07:07 N19 Before Osiris

区分：myth_variant

According to the museum, if the scale balanced, the deceased was accepted by Osiris and granted eternal life. If not, the heart was devoured by a creature called Ammut, and the person died forever. Anubis weighs, Thoth records, Osiris receives. Judgment was pictured as a scene involving many gods.

## 07:30 N20 Beyond the fear

区分：editorial_interpretation

The judgment scene does have its frightening side. But the sources prepared for the dead were not about failure alone. They were about passing the test safely and moving on to the next existence. Seen through his role as a protector, the same scene of the scale begins to look a little different.
