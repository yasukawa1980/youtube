The great west gate of the Tang dynasty capital Luoyang in ancient China, on a spring evening. A massive old gate tower of dark timber and grey tiled roofs with gently upturned eaves stands on a thick earthen and brick city wall, with one wide arched passage. The broad empty stone street in front of the gate is bathed in thick, oily, golden evening sunlight coming from the left, casting long shadows. The sky is a soft hazy spring blue fading to gold, and high in it hangs one very thin, pale crescent moon, like a fingernail mark. A faint spring haze softens the distance. This is Chinese architecture, not Japanese: no torii, no Japanese shrines, no pagodas. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No horses, no carts. Colours: honey gold, warm vermilion-brown timber, grey tiles, soft hazy blue. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no electric lamps, no vehicles, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
