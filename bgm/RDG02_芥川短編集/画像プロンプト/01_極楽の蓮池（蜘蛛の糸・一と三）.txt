The lotus pond of the Buddhist paradise (gokuraku), seen from just above the water at its edge. The still water is almost entirely covered with large round lotus leaves of soft jade green, and among them stand many lotus flowers, every one pure white like polished jewels, each with a golden centre. The far bank is lost in a soft luminous haze; there are no buildings, no temples, no pagodas, no bridges, no boats, no stone lanterns. It is early morning in paradise: the whole sky and the haze are a pale, clear gold-and-cream light, softly gradated, and the white flowers glow gently in it. A faint, fragrant calm hangs over the water. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No Buddha, no deity figures. Colours: pale gold, cream white, soft jade green, a little pale aqua in the water. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no electric lamps, no vehicles, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
