The lotus pond of the Buddhist paradise (gokuraku), seen from just above the water at its edge. The still water is almost entirely covered with large round lotus leaves of soft jade green, and among them stand many lotus flowers, every one pure white like polished jewels, each with a golden centre. The far bank is lost in a soft luminous haze; there are no buildings, no temples, no pagodas, no bridges, no boats, no stone lanterns. It is the quiet hour just before dawn in paradise: the sky is a deep, soft blue-violet, softly gradated, and only the far horizon behind the haze holds one thin, faint band of pale gold, softly reflected in the still water. The white flowers glow gently as if lit from within, clearly readable against the darker leaves. A faint, fragrant calm hangs over the water. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No Buddha, no deity figures. Colours: deep blue-violet, soft indigo-green leaves, glowing pure white flowers with golden centres, one thin line of pale gold at the horizon. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no electric lamps, no vehicles, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
