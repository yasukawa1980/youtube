The same narrow light railway at dusk, now seen as a lonely path home: the thin rails run toward us along the edge of a hillside, past a dark thicket of bamboo on the right, and lead away into a small village in a hollow far ahead, where a few windows of low tiled houses glow with warm yellow lamplight. The sky is a deep violet-blue with the last thin band of fading orange sunset above the distant hills on the left, and the first one or two stars. The trolley is not in this scene. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No cherry blossoms, no autumn leaves, no snow. Colours: deep violet-blue, fading orange, dark green-black bamboo, small warm yellow window lights; the rails and the path stay clearly visible. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no cars, no trucks, no trains, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
