High on Mount Emei in ancient China, late at night. In the foreground, a wide, flat single slab of rock on the edge of a deep valley; behind it, a sheer cliff face from which one old, twisted pine grows out, its branches stirring in the night wind. Beyond, rows of steep, sharp mountain peaks fade into blue mist layer after layer. The sky is a deep clear indigo, and the seven stars of the Big Dipper hang huge and bright in it, each about the size of a teacup, with many small quiet stars around them. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No tigers, no snakes, no dragons, no gods, no warriors, no lightning, no fire. Colours: deep indigo, cool blue-grey rock and mist, dark green pine, the Big Dipper stars clearly bright; the rock slab and the pine stay clearly readable. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no electric lamps, no vehicles, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
