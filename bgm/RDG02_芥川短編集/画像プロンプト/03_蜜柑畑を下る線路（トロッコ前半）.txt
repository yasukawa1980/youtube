A narrow-gauge light railway of the Taisho era running down a gentle hillside between terraced mandarin orange groves in late winter, near the sea on the Izu coast. In the middle distance on the thin curving rails stands one small, empty, open wooden mine trolley (a low flat wooden cart on four small iron wheels, the kind pushed by hand). The orange trees are dark glossy green and full of small bright orange fruit. The rails curve down and away toward the lower left. Beyond the hill, far below, a calm pale sea opens to the horizon. Late afternoon light, low and warm, with long soft shadows. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No cherry blossoms, no autumn leaves, no snow. Colours: deep green, warm orange dots of fruit, honey-gold light, pale blue-grey sea. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no cars, no trucks, no trains, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
