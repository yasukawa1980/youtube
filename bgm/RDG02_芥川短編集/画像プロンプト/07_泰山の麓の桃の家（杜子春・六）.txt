At the southern foot of Mount Tai in ancient China, in spring. A single small, simple farmhouse with whitewashed earthen walls and a grey tiled roof stands among fields, and all around it peach trees are in full bloom, a soft cloud of pink blossom surrounding the house. A narrow footpath leads to the house from the lower left. Behind, the gentle slopes of the mountain rise as a dark blue silhouette. It is dusk on a spring evening: the sun has just set behind the ridge, the sky is a deep dusky blue fading to a soft rose-orange glow low above the mountain, the peach blossoms are softened into dusky pink, and one small window of the farmhouse glows with warm yellow lamplight. Peaceful, quiet and kind. This is Chinese countryside, not Japanese: no torii, no shrines, no pagodas. No people, no figures, no faces, no hands, no animals, no birds, no insects, no cats. No cherry blossoms (the blossoms are peach blossoms, rounder and deeper pink, on bare dark branches). Colours: dusky blue, soft rose-orange afterglow, muted peach pink, dim cream walls, one small warm yellow window light. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no utility poles, no electric lamps, no vehicles, no plastic. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
