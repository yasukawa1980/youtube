A long country path through quiet fields on a bright moonlit night in rural Japan in the 1930s. The path runs between low hedges and a few pines toward a far village where the lights of a night festival glow: a row of red and white paper lanterns, the roof of a small shrine, a faint glow of a festival float. The moon is full and high; the path is pale in its light and long shadows of the pines fall across it. Colours: deep blue night, silver moonlight, small red and warm festival lights in the distance. Most of the image is dark, dreamy and a little mysterious. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere.
