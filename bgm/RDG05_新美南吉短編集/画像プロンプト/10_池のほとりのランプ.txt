A large still pond at night in the Japanese countryside in spring, on a windless night. Along the far bank stand old trees and willows, and from their branches hang dozens of lit glass kerosene lamps, large and small, big and little, all burning quietly without a flicker, so the bank is bright as day. Every lamp is reflected perfectly in the black mirror of the water as a second row of lights. A dark road runs along the near shore. Colours: deep blue-black night, many warm golden lights doubled in the water, dark willows. Beautiful, solemn and very still. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. No foxes, no dogs, no animals of any kind anywhere in the image. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
