A narrow street of a small Japanese town in the 1920s on a snowy winter night. Low wooden shops with closed shutters line both sides, their eaves heavy with snow. Above each shop hangs a wooden signboard lit by one small lamp. One shop has a signboard shaped like a large black silk top hat, and its door is open a crack so that a long band of warm yellow light falls out across the white snow of the street. Snow falls softly. A small fox cub stands at the edge of the light, looking up. From one window above, soft warm light glows behind a paper screen. Colours: deep blue night, white snow, warm golden door light, black top hat sign, russet cub. Tender and hushed. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Any fox is a real wild Japanese red fox drawn simply and gently in the print manner, small in the scene, never cartoonish, never wearing clothes. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
