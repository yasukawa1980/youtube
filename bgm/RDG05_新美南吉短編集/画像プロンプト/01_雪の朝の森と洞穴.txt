A quiet winter wood in rural Japan after a night of heavy snow, at the very first light of morning before sunrise, drawn as a blue dawn scene and not as bright daytime. Deep fresh snow covers the ground and weighs down the branches of fir and bare trees; a little powder snow drifts down from a branch like silver silk thread. At the foot of a snowy bank is the dark round mouth of a fox den, and beside it a small fox cub stands with its nose lifted toward the snow, its mother half visible in the den mouth. The snow is pale blue with soft rose light on the tops of the drifts, and the sky is pale rose fading to blue-grey. Colours: pale blue snow, soft rose light, dark fir green, warm russet fox fur. Gentle and fresh. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Any fox is a real wild Japanese red fox drawn simply and gently in the print manner, small in the scene, never cartoonish, never wearing clothes. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
