A country lane in a Japanese village on a bright moonlit autumn night. A full moon hangs high in a deep blue sky. The narrow earth road runs past a small thatched farmhouse whose door stands open onto a dark earth-floored entry; on the doorstep lies a small heap of chestnuts in their burrs, gleaming in the moonlight. Pines and the dark shape of a small hill castle in the distance. Crickets seem to sing in the grass. Colours: deep blue, silver moonlight, warm brown chestnuts, dark thatch. Most of the image is dark, gentle and lonely. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Any fox is a real wild Japanese red fox drawn simply and gently in the print manner, small in the scene, never cartoonish, never wearing clothes. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
