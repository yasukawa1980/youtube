An old Japanese village graveyard on a gentle hillside in early autumn, in the late afternoon with long soft shadows. Small weathered stone grave markers and a row of six little stone Jizo statues stand among the grass. Across the slope and along the edge of the rice fields below, red spider lilies bloom in a long blazing carpet, like spread red cloth. Far off, the thatched roofs of the village and the low tiled roof of a small temple, and a faint ringing feeling in the air. The sky is pale and calm. Colours: vivid red lilies, soft grey stone, gold-green fields, pale sky. Still and quietly sad. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Any fox is a real wild Japanese red fox drawn simply and gently in the print manner, small in the scene, never cartoonish, never wearing clothes. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
