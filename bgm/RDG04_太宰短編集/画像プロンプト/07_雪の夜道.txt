A quiet residential lane near Kichijoji, Tokyo, in the 1940s, on a snowy night. Deep fresh snow covers the narrow road and the low wooden fences; the trees on both sides bow under heavy snow. Snowflakes fall thickly and softly through the light of a single old street lamp, swirling like countless fireflies. A small red post box stands capped with snow. A row of footprints runs along the road. Colours: deep blue night, soft white snow, warm yellow lamplight, one small red post box. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
