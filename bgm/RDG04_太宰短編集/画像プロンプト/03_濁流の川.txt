A wide river in flood among wooded hills under a heavy dark rain sky. The muddy torrent surges in great curling waves drawn in the bold stylised manner of Japanese woodblock prints, carrying broken branches. The remains of a wooden bridge are gone; only two broken stumps of piers stand in the water. On the far bank a narrow mountain road climbs up into dark pines toward a pass. Rain falls in fine slanting lines. Colours: dark slate blue, ochre-brown water with pale foam, black-green pines. Powerful but still drawn calmly, nothing gory. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
