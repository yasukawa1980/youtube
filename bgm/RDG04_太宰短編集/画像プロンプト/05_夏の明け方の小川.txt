A quiet small town in Izu, Japan, in the 1930s, on a summer night in the last hour before dawn, drawn as a true night scene and not as a darkened daytime scene. The sky is deep indigo with a few fading stars and a low pale moon; there is no sunlight anywhere. Seen from the dark wooden veranda of an old Japanese house: beyond a low hedge, a meadow of tall summer grass and a little stream running full and slow, catching silver moonlight, with a narrow earth path along it. A few small fireflies glow green-gold above the stream. Thin mist lies low over the water. Colours: deep indigo, blue-black grass, silver moonlit water, tiny green-gold lights, dark wood veranda. Most of the image is dark, calm and quietly happy. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
