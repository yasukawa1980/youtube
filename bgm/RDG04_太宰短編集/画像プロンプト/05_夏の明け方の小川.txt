A quiet small town in Izu, Japan, in the 1930s, at early summer dawn, drawn as a blue-hour scene and not as a bright day. Seen from the wooden veranda of an old Japanese house: beyond a low hedge, a green meadow of tall summer grass and a clear little stream running full and slow, with a narrow earth path along it. The path is empty. Morning mist lies low over the water, and a pale lemon-pink light rises behind distant hills. Colours: soft blue-green, pale mist, lemon-pink sky, dark wood veranda. Fresh, calm and quietly happy. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
