Late afternoon toward sunset after the rain, drawn as an evening scene. In the foreground a small clear spring trickles out of a cleft in a mossy rock beside a forest road, glinting. Beyond the trees the road runs downhill across fields toward a far walled city on a hill, its towers small and dark against the sky. The setting sun throws long low red-gold light through the leaves so that they glow as if on fire, and the sky above is deep crimson fading to violet. Colours: crimson and gold light, violet sky, dark green-black forest, a small bright spring. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
