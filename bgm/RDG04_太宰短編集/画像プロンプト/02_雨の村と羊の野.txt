A small farming village of ancient Sicily deep in a rainy night just before dawn, drawn as a true night scene and not as a darkened daytime scene. The sky is a heavy deep blue-black with low rain clouds and no sun at all; only the faintest grey-blue glow lies along the far hills. Low stone farmhouses with tiled roofs, a simple stone sheepfold with a few pale sheep huddled under its roof, an olive tree, and a dark meadow running to low hills. Thin slanting lines of rain fill the air in the woodblock manner, and dark pools on the path catch reflections. One farmhouse window glows warm yellow, the brightest spot in the image, as if a small celebration is going on inside. Colours: deep blue-black, dark slate, pale sheep, one warm yellow window. Most of the image is dark. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
