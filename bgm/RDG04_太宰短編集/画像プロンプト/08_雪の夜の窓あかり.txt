A small old wooden Japanese house at night in deep snow, seen from the garden. Its sliding glass doors glow with warm yellow light from inside, falling in soft rectangles across the snow. Snow is gently falling, the trees and the roof are heavy with white, and the night sky is deep indigo. Inside, only a low table and a lamp are suggested through the glass, no one visible. Colours: deep indigo, soft white snow, warm golden window light. Tender, warm and very calm. No people, no figures, no silhouettes of people, no faces, no hands, no animals except those named above, no cats. Japanese shin-hanga woodblock print in the manner of the 1920s-30s revival: flat layered areas of colour, visible woodgrain in the flat passages, softly gradated sky (bokashi), Western perspective and cast light held inside Japanese printmaking. Everything in the scene is drawn as part of the print, in the same flat woodblock manner, never photographic. Calm, dreamlike and hushed. Strong clear difference between the light and dark masses so the image still reads when reduced to a thumbnail. No text, no signature, no seal stamp, no lettering of any kind. No modern objects: no power lines, no cars, no trucks, no plastic, no electric signs. Wide 16:9 landscape. Exposed so that texture is clearly visible everywhere. Shadows are lifted and open, full of visible detail, nothing crushed to pure black. High dynamic range, rich texture even in the darkest areas.
