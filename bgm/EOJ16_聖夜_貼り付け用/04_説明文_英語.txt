Thirteen Christmas carols and hymns, slowed down and spread across two hours on koto,
shakuhachi and sho.

The two hours open and close with Silent Night, first sung in 1818 in a village church in
Austria to Gruber's guitar. In between come the old carols in the
order of one Christmas Eve: dusk with O Come, All Ye Faithful and Good King Wenceslas; the
deep of the night with Greensleeves, O Come, O Come, Emmanuel and Lo, How a Rose E'er
Blooming; midnight with O Holy Night, It Came Upon the Midnight Clear and Hark! The Herald
Angels Sing; and Christmas morning with Joy to the World and Bach's In Dulci Jubilo. At the
end, Silent Night returns, slower than at the start.

There is no voice, no organ and no piano here. The notes are the composers', but each pitch
has been moved to its nearest neighbour in a five-tone Japanese scale, the tempo is far below
the written one, and the sound is made by plucked strings and breath. The soprano line goes
to the shakuhachi, the koto or the sho; the lower voices go to the koto and to the sho's held
chords underneath. Most carols are major-key tunes, so each was given the Japanese scale that
keeps the most of its melody — for Silent Night, nine notes out of ten stay where they were.

Each of the sixteen pieces changes the scale, the tonic, the tempo and which instrument
carries the tune, and they are joined by eight-second crossfades, so the music never stops
and restarts.

Three turn dark, at the bottom of the night. They use miyakobushi, the Japanese pentatonic
that has semitones — the shadowed one. One of them is Silent Night itself, heard in that
shadowed scale. Four pieces carry no melody at all — only their slow chords.

The pictures follow one Christmas Eve on a small island cove in western Japan, where an old
wooden church with a tiled roof stands above the fishing boats: the window lit at dusk, the
darkest hour under the stars, the first snow falling as the door glows gold, and a white
morning with snow on the pines.

Public domain music, newly arranged. Scores from the Mutopia Project (public domain
engravings). Only pitch and duration were used.
The instruments are synthesised rather than recorded — there is no player and no
microphone. The still images were made with AI.
