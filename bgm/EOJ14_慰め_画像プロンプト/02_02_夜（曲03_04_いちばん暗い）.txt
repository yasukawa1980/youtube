The same path through the old grove of wild camellia trees, deep in a moonless winter night: the darkest scene. The grove is an old wood of wild Japanese camellia trees (yabu-tsubaki, Camellia japonica) on a quiet hillside in Japan: smooth pale grey trunks, dense dark glossy evergreen leaves, and single deep-red five-petalled flowers, each with a short cup of golden stamens, not roses, not peonies, not double flowers. A narrow earthen path covered in soft green moss winds through the grove, and whole fallen camellia flowers lie face-up on the moss, still perfectly red and intact. The trees close in on both sides and overhead, which fills the background and shuts out any further view: no distant mountains, no sea, no lake, no far horizon. The path runs from the lower left into the middle distance. Deep indigo, night blue, blue-green, dark wine-red, silver-grey on the trunks. Quiet and still, but nothing lost in black: every leaf and trunk is still faintly visible.
